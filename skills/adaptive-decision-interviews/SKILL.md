# Adaptive Decision Interviews

Use for an explicitly requested interview that preserves the human’s judgement before an authorised decision artefact. Do not activate for ordinary chat, advice, research, brainstorming, one-off clarification or execution once direction is clear.

## Ordinary path

1. Read the available sources first. Recover facts yourself. Establish the decision being supported, current phase and authorised output in a short orientation, using existing context rather than asking for ceremonial assent.
2. Ask **one useful human-only question** whose different answers could materially change that output. Ask about judgement, trade-offs, ownership or acceptable consequences, not facts you can retrieve. Permit rough bullets, examples, “none” and “I don’t know”.
3. Preserve the answer verbatim before interpreting it. Keep the human’s words separate from agent inference, source facts and assistant-proposed framing. Assent to your framing alone is weak evidence; seek an actual criterion or episode when load-bearing judgement is still missing.
4. Apply corrections immediately without rewriting history. Keep uncertainty visible; name what could resolve it or deliberately defer it. Recompute the next useful question rather than following a fixed script.
5. Stop when another answer would not materially change the authorised output. Give the supported direction and unresolved/deferred matters. Do not force every uncertainty into a choice.

## Phase boundaries

The human owns the decision. An interview does not authorise research expansion, a proposal, drafting, publication or implementation. Keep the decision horizon distinct from the example used to orient it. If the human challenges the purpose or says they want to direct the work, pause the pending question and resolve that boundary first.

On pause, preserve the current direction and open questions; do not leave a pending question or continue without resume. On closure, say whether the interview is complete or merely complete enough for a named artefact. Crossing into drafting requires authorisation, even when the agent thinks it is ready. A publishable interview record and a decision artefact informed by it are separate outputs.

## Deeper records only when needed

For a durable multi-phase interview, use [extended record protocol](references/extended-record-protocol.md) for explicit contract/state/register mechanics. Consult [evidence provenance](references/evidence-and-elicitation-provenance.md), [phase transitions](references/phase-transitions-and-readiness.md) or [implementation semantics](references/decision-separation-and-implementation-semantics.md) only for the relevant boundary. Do not impose the extended protocol on an ordinary short interview.

## Check

Is this question consequential, human-only, current and low-burden? Is the answer preserved separately from inference? Is uncertainty honest, and is the next phase authorised? If no useful question remains, stop.
