# Subtractive prose revision reviews

Use this reference when an editing pass removes AI-shaped cadence, rhetorical polish, repetition, or length from public technical prose. The review question is whether subtraction preserved the article's intellectual and teaching quality—not whether detector-style counts improved.

## Brief the exact comparison

Bind the reviewer to immutable base and candidate revisions plus the exact artefact path. Let it inspect the complete candidate, but keep previous detector reports and editorial commentary outside the source boundary so the reviewer judges the prose rather than echoing the diagnosis.

State that punctuation and construction counts are observations, not acceptance criteria. A lower count of em dashes, contrasts, fragments, or signposts is not evidence of better writing.

## Falsification probes

Ask the reviewer to find concrete cases where the revision:

- removed a technical qualification, prohibition, ownership boundary, or failure condition;
- preserved a fact but weakened the rule readers should remember;
- flattened voice, humour, rhythm, or a useful callback;
- made transitions choppy or references ambiguous;
- removed a teaching move or mnemonic without replacing its function;
- introduced repetition, awkward grammar, vagueness, or overclaiming; or
- became less useful to the intended technical reader when read as a whole.

Also require the reviewer to name conspicuous subtractions that are genuine improvements and should stay. This prevents an adversarial brief from rewarding restoration merely because the original sounded polished.

## Report shape

Require:

- one overall judgement;
- Critical, Important, and Minor findings;
- exact before/after quotations for each finding;
- the concrete lost function, not a generic taste claim;
- the smallest correction;
- separation of technical regressions from authorial preferences; and
- one verdict: `PASS`, `PASS WITH MINOR CORRECTIONS`, or `REQUEST CHANGES`.

Critical and Important findings block acceptance. Minor voice choices remain the author's call.

## Controller validation

Treat the report as leads, not instructions. For each finding:

1. verify the quoted base and candidate text at the named revisions;
2. check the technical premise against the candidate's surrounding context;
3. distinguish an actual lost contract from a reviewer preference for the old phrasing;
4. preserve valid wording criticism even if part of the reviewer's causal explanation is wrong; and
5. recommend only corrections whose restored function outweighs reintroducing the original cadence.

A strong outcome may be “the pass improved the article, with a handful of local repairs”. Do not turn that into a wholesale rollback or another global humanisation pass.
