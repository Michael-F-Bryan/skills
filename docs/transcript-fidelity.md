# Transcript fidelity reference

Acceptance lessons only—not an executor or a workflow skill.

- Keep original recordings/transcripts immutable, with source identity and timing. A notes-only PDF is not evidence of dialogue; never fabricate a transcript from it.
- Preserve substantive claims, objections, alternatives, names, numbers, speaker order and uncertainty. Structural validity or word-retention percentages do not establish fidelity.
- Human corrections outrank diarisation guesses. Keep uncertain turns unresolved; never name a silent attendee by elimination. Honour requested speaker-review checkpoints before downstream work.
- Chapters should follow topic/turn boundaries and cover source turns once, without gaps, duplication or cross-speaker merges. Account for intentional filler/duplicate drops.
- Read representative early, middle and late passages against the source. Readability edits must not turn dialogue into summary or erase qualifications.
- Minutes distinguish proposals, objections, tentative agreement, decisions, actual commitments and unresolved questions. The longest-spoken proposal is not consensus.
- Corrections invalidate dependent chapters, minutes and renders. Recheck affected outputs against the corrected source and exact final revision.
- Verify source links and final written output at the recipient boundary. Missing canonical output means incomplete, not success.
